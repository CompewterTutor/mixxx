Create a plan for a feature that allows mixxx to have peer-to-peer networking with super low latency. A pair of DJs can fire up Mixxx and open a browser dialog box where they can connect to a peer's IP/port and mix tracks with them - Tracks will be cached on both peers before being allowed to route live sound from a channel it's assigned to. Track can be assigned and queued and that is when a background action will send the track to the other peer so that it can live in the remote cache (can be encrypted with an optional but default rotating pub/private key scheme only decryptable during a live session with the peer who owns the track.) No sound will be transferred outside potentially a voice chat channel between the DJs, and channels can be assigned "owners" ahead of time or open channels can be locked with a mutex-like reservation to prevent clobbering and race conditions on the channels. Only knob movements are sent over the wire along with a synced timer, movements can optionally be quantized down to 64th notes and the movements are played back on each local instance so that there's no audio delay and mixing basically all happens locally. I'd like to model the networking after the popular method in fighting games called rollback networking, so that there's an expected amount of rollback frames and it only rolls back knob and fader movements and transport controls. if we rollback frames and there's a change in fader movements, smooth interpolation will be the default manner of reconciliation.
